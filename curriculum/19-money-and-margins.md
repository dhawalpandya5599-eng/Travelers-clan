# Lesson 19: Money, margins and the books

GST on domestic tour packages is five percent.
Tour operators charge GST without input tax credit.
Prices should be quoted GST inclusive to customers.
A healthy group trip margin is eighteen to twenty five percent of price.
Margin is profit divided by price.
Markup is profit divided by cost.
Fixed costs are the bus, the captain, the permits and the marketing.
Variable costs are stay, meals and activities per head.
Break even seats equal fixed costs divided by contribution per seat.
Contribution per seat is price minus variable cost per head.
A batch should launch six weeks before departure.
A batch under half full ten days out should merge or push.
The organiser offer is one free seat per ten paid seats.
The referral reward is five percent off for both sides.
Hotels want twenty five percent to block rooms.
Hotel balance is due at check in or seven days before.
Bus operators want fifty percent advance for a fixed departure.
Vendor payments should go only after a written quote.
Every booking needs a GST invoice.
Every refund needs a written note of the reason.
Cash collections should be deposited the same day.
A separate bank account should hold customer advances.
Advances are not revenue until the trip runs.
Price per person should be rounded to the nearest five hundred.
A twenty percent margin on eleven thousand cost is thirteen thousand seven hundred fifty price.
Discounts should come as added value, not cut price.
Added value is a bonfire night, an airport pickup or a room upgrade.
A price cut on remaining seats loses revenue on every seat.
Pay in parts is the tool for fence sitters.
A hold without advance should expire in twenty four hours.
Monthly reports should show leads, bookings, revenue and margin per batch.
Cost per booking is ad spend divided by bookings.
Customer lifetime value is repeat trips times average margin.
A repeat traveller costs nothing to acquire.
