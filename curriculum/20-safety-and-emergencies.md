# Lesson 20: Safety and emergencies

The emergency number in India is 112.
The ambulance number in India is 108.
The women helpline number in India is 1091.
The tourist helpline number in India is 1363.
Every trip needs a first aid kit with ORS, bandages and painkillers.
Every traveller needs an emergency contact on file.
Every traveller needs a medical declaration before an altitude trip.
Altitude above two thousand five hundred metres needs an acclimatisation day.
Altitude sickness signs are headache, nausea and breathlessness.
The cure for altitude sickness is descent.
A pulse oximeter reading under eighty five percent means descend.
Alcohol is banned for the first two days at altitude.
Travellers should drink three litres of water a day at altitude.
Diamox is taken only on a doctor's advice.
Blood pressure patients need a doctor's clearance for Ladakh and Spiti.
Asthma patients need their inhaler and a doctor's clearance at altitude.
Pregnant travellers should avoid altitude and rough roads.
Kids under eight should avoid Spiti and high passes.
Night driving on mountain roads is banned for our vehicles.
Drivers must rest after every four hours of driving.
Every vehicle needs a spare tyre, a tow rope and a torch.
Every route needs a backup vehicle contact.
The captain needs the hospital list for the route.
A lost traveller is reported to police after thirty minutes.
Women travellers are roomed only with women.
Every women only departure needs a female captain.
Swimming is allowed only at lifeguard beaches.
Rafting needs a life jacket and a helmet.
Trek start time is seven in the morning.
River crossings after rain are banned on monsoon treks.
The founder's number is live twenty four hours during a trip.
An incident log entry is written the same day.
Travel insurance cover is trip interruption and medical evacuation.
Insurance costs about one percent of the trip price.
