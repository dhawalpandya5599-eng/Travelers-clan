# Lesson 27: Pricing strategies and analytics

The early bird price is seven percent below standard.
The early bird tier is the first thirty percent of seats.
The early bird window end is four weeks before departure.
The standard price is the price on the website.
The last three seats are never discounted.
The premium tier is twenty five percent above standard.
The premium tier is a decoy that makes standard look right.
The price floor is ten percent above ground cost.
A competitor price is answered with inclusion match, not a cut.
A price cut on remaining seats loses margin on every seat.
A price cut is a lesson to customers to wait.
A batch over eighty percent full means raise the next batch by five to eight percent.
A price should not rise mid sale.
High demand means a second batch, not a higher price.
Group discounts are replaced by the organiser seat.
Price per day is price divided by days.
Implied margin is price per day minus ground cost per day divided by price per day.
Implied margin under fifteen percent is thin.
Implied margin over thirty five percent is a cause of objections.
Quote to book rate is bookings divided by quoted leads.
Quote to book rate under twenty percent means test pay in parts.
Revenue per batch is booked seats times price.
Open revenue is seats left times price.
Run rate is seats left divided by days to departure.
Anchoring is showing the premium tier before standard.
Scarcity is the real seat count on screen.
Instalments raise conversion for price sensitive buyers.
Added value raises perceived price without cost.
Pricing is reviewed after every batch.
