# Lesson 3: Leads and conversion

A lead is a person who messaged about a trip.
A hot lead is a lead with a travel date, a destination, a budget and a group size.
A warm lead is a lead with intent but missing a date or a budget.
A cold lead is a lead who only asked the price.
A junk lead is a lead with no intent to travel.
The first reply should go out within ten minutes.
A fast first reply doubles the chance of a booking.
The first reply should ask for the travel date, the group size and the budget.
A qualifying question is a question that reveals date, budget or group size.
Price should be shared only after the dates and group size are known.
A lead who asks only for the price needs a qualifying question first.
A follow-up is a message to a lead who went quiet.
The first follow-up should go out after 24 hours.
The second follow-up should go out after 3 days.
The third follow-up should offer a new angle such as a group discount or a date change.
Ghosting is when a lead stops replying after the price.
Ghosting usually means the price felt high or the plan felt vague.
A voice note builds more trust than a long text.
A short itinerary PDF increases replies.
Social proof is photos and reviews from past trips.
Social proof should be sent before the price.
An advance is the payment that confirms a booking.
The advance should be collected on the same day the lead says yes.
A payment link reduces drop-off at the advance step.
A cancellation-proof offer is a flexible date change instead of a refund.
A WhatsApp broadcast should go to past travelers before a new trip opens.
Past travelers are the cheapest source of new bookings.
A referral is a booking that came from a past traveler.
Referrals should be rewarded with a discount on the next trip.
Conversion rate is bookings divided by leads.
A good conversion rate for group trips is five to ten percent.
